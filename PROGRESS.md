**Phase 3 fix — real-device feedback (2026-07-05):** you reported the title-log gesture wasn't working — it seemed to get treated as a normal highlight drag instead. Root cause: the movement tolerance for "is this still a hold" was too tight (4% of frame height) for what a real held finger naturally does — a thumb held in place for ~450ms while also gripping the phone drifts more than that from ordinary hand tremor, which was silently cancelling the hold and letting it fall through to a normal drag-capture. Split into two separate, looser thresholds in `CaptureView.jsx`: `DOUBLE_TAP_POSITION_THRESHOLD` (8%, for matching the two taps' positions) and `HOLD_MOVE_CANCEL_THRESHOLD` (12%, for how much the finger can drift during the hold itself before it's treated as a drag) — the hold-cancel threshold is deliberately looser since sustained holding wobbles more than a quick tap misses its target. Re-verified: the hold now fires correctly even with simulated wobble around the old threshold's boundary, and a normal drag-capture still fires correctly (no regression). Please retry logging a title on your phone — if it still doesn't trigger reliably, the next thing to check would be whether iOS/Android's own long-press behaviors (text-selection magnifier, etc.) are intercepting the gesture before our touch handlers see it, which `touch-action: none` should already prevent but is worth confirming.

**Phase 3 revision 3 — real-device feedback (2026-07-06):** three issues from testing the previous revision:

1. **iOS's native text-selection magnifier was intercepting the hold gesture** — a sustained press-and-hold, even inside our touch handlers, is exactly what triggers Safari's built-in long-press-to-select UI, which was grabbing the gesture before it could reliably reach our JS. Rather than fight the OS for control of a hold, **replaced the double-tap-and-hold gesture with a plain triple-tap (no hold at all)** — three quick taps in the same spot (`TRIPLE_TAP_WINDOW_MS = 400`, `TRIPLE_TAP_POSITION_THRESHOLD = 0.08`), tracked via a small rolling history of the last two taps in `CaptureView.jsx`. Since there's no sustained press anywhere in the interaction anymore, there's nothing for the native magnifier to latch onto. Also added `select-none` and `-webkit-touch-callout:none` to the capture surface as defense-in-depth. All the hold-specific code (`holdTimerRef`, `HOLD_DURATION_MS`, `HOLD_MOVE_CANCEL_THRESHOLD`, etc.) is gone.

2. **The title capture rectangle could come out landscape-shaped** — the previous design centered the box on the tap's Y position and let it get clamped against the top/bottom edge of the frame, which silently shrank just the height while width stayed fixed, producing a wide/flat box if you tapped near the top of the screen (as one often does on a title page). Fixed by decoupling the capture region from the tap position entirely: `getTitleCaptureBounds()` in `lib/capture.js` now always returns a **fixed, centered, portrait rectangle** (80% width × 70% height of frame, which — since the camera frame itself is portrait — produces a genuinely portrait-shaped crop, verified at 3428×3998px against a real frame). No more clamping-induced distortion.

**Phase 4 (2026-07-06):** New `lib/continuation.js` — `isCandidateContinuation` (pure heuristic: prev passage's `selectionBounds.yMax > 0.85`, new passage's `selectionBounds.yMin < 0.15`, prev's `refinedText` missing terminal punctuation `[.!?]`; at least 2 of 3 required, per spec) and `maybeMergeWithPrevious` (orchestration: pulls the immediately-preceding passage from storage, runs the heuristic, calls `checkContinuation` only if flagged, and merges via `replacePassages` if confirmed — wrapped in try/catch so any failure leaves both passages separate, the safe default). Wired into `CaptureView.jsx`'s `performCapture` as a fire-and-forget call right after save — entirely invisible either way, no toast, no prompt, per spec.

Found and fixed a real bug while verifying: the merged entry's `mergedFromIds` was computed as `prevPassage.mergedFromIds ?? [prevPassage.id]`, but a fresh (non-merged) passage's `mergedFromIds` is always `[]` — never `null`/`undefined` — so `??` never fell through to `[prevPassage.id]`, and the previous passage's own id silently vanished from the merge chain. Fixed by branching on `prevPassage.isMerged` instead of on `mergedFromIds` being empty.
